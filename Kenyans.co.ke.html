High Court Declares 2% HIMS Fee on SHA Claims Unconstitutional

  • by Kenyans.co.ke Writer on Tuesday, 24 November 2026 - 8:17 am

The High Court in Vihiga has declared the 2 per cent Health Information Management Service (HIMS) fee deducted from Social Health Authority (SHA) claims unconstitutional.

In a judgment delivered virtually on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, Justice Reuben Nyakundi ruled that the fee, which is capped at Ksh5,000 per claim under the Digital Health (Data Exchange Component) Regulations, 2025, was not subjected to public participation.

The judge faulted the government for not involving Kenyans on its rate, cap, or formula, and not seeking Senate concurrence on devolved health functions. 

"The fee was never put to the public as a fee, with its amount and its formula, before it was made. That failure is fatal to the fee in its present form, and it is the foundation of the declarations which may flow as a consequence of the determination of this petition," Justice Nyakundi ruled.

The ruling follows a petition filed by Nakuru-based surgeon Dr Benjamin Magare-Gikenyi, Eliud Karanja Matindi, and Busia Senator Okiya Omtatah Okoiti. The three had moved to court, arguing that the fee was illegal and a form of double taxation.
 
The petitioners told the court that the 2 per cent HIMS fee lacked statutory authority and parliamentary approval and was imposed on Kenyans without public participation.
 
Justice Nyakundi heard that the deduction violates the principles of public finance under Article 201 of the Constitution, entails the unlawful diversion of public funds to a private entity, the 2nd Respondent, Finsprint Limited, and infringes patients' right to privacy under Article 31 of the Constitution.
 
SHA, on the other hand, maintained that the fee is a lawful charge for services rendered under Article 209(4) of the Constitution, with its statutory foundation in Section 48(1)(c) of the Digital Health Act, 2023, and Regulation 11(2) of, together with the Third Schedule to, the Digital Health (Data Exchange Component) Regulations, 2025 (Legal Notice No. 77 of 2025).
 
Countering the petitioners' argument that public money worth over Ksh1.2 deducted towards healthcare had ended up in “private pockets”, SHA contended that the fee is payable to a public body and is duly accounted for as public money.
 
The petitioners asked the court to declare the 2 per cent HIMS fee unconstitutional and order a refund of all monies deducted within 90 days.
 
The court found that an immediate invalidation would remove, overnight, a funding stream for a system that processes some 26,000 claims a day, with the disruption to facilities and patients. 
 
"A refund of fees on hundreds of thousands of claims would be disproportionate to a procedural defect, and the record does not identify who paid what. The declaration operates prospectively," the judge argued.
 
The judge found that the defect was procedural and can be cured by suspending the declaration of invalidity for 120 days.
 
"A refund of fees on hundreds of thousands of claims would be disproportionate to a procedural defect, and the record does not identify who paid what. The declaration operates prospectively," the judge added.

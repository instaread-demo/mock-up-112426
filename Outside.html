Salomon S/Pro Alpha C BOA Ski Boot

Some boots turn heads with new gizmos. The S/Pro Alpha C BOA draws attention with what’s missing: the traditional cuff pivot at the inner ankle. Instead, the cuff flows seamlessly into a low-mounted Powerlink plate—smooth, screw-free, almost Silver Surfer-esque. It’s a radical break from overlap construction, and it delivers two big wins: shockingly easy entry for a narrow boot, and an on-snow feel like nothing else in the category. That low Powerlink connection creates a direct line from leg to ski—sensitive and reactive enough that we pushed harder into edge angles, hips dropping closer to the snow than in any other boot we tested. This one’s for skiers who want a snug, locked-in fit and live for the high-performance turn.

Mind Your Pistes and Queues

Five things every skier and rider should already know—but some need to be reminded.

By Jenny Wiegand

  1. Rack your skis. That’s it. That’s the whole ask. Skis left sprawled across the snow like a game of pick-up sticks outside the lodge tend to meet the business end of a crusty local’s snowplow—and for good reason.
  2. Alternate lift lanes. And do it all the way across, not just the first two rows. Yes, even without a liftie playing traffic cop. Think of it like a group project: when everyone contributes, everyone gets on the lift faster.
  3. Stop with some sense. The middle of a run or a narrow cat track is the perfect place for exactly nothing—not for regrouping, not for catching your breath, not for taking a picture. Pull over where uphill traffic can see you and you’re not blocking everyone else’s line—then you can take as many breaths and all the pictures you want.
  4. Call “bar!” Do pull the safety bar down. Don’t pull it down without giving fellow riders a heads up. Helmets are better than ever, but they still weren’t designed to protect heads from being bashed by a steel bar.
  5. Pocket the vape. Save the celebratory rip, the beer, the whatever-else for a chair without a kid on it. Nobody’s judging your style—just your timing.

Salomon Highpath Snowboard

Revamped for 2027 with input from pro rider Nils Mindnich, the Highpath now features a rounded silhouette that floats better in powder and rides switch better, too. Snow-Proof Your Trip

How to plan a ski vacation that survives a low-snow winter.

By Samantha Berman

  • Choose a snow-sure resort. It’s always smart to stack the odds in your favor. Focus on resorts at higher altitudes with colder climates and historically reliable snowfall to improve your odds of good coverage even in a bad snow year.
  • Look for diverse terrain. Home in on resorts with lots of varied aspects and terrain pods. More terrain means more options when snow quality varies.
  • Keep lodging flexible. Whenever possible, book refundable lodging. If the forecast tanks, you’ll have the freedom to pivot at the last minute.
  • Follow the snow. Wait as long as you can to book so you can see where the storms are headed, and follow their path.
  • Pick a resort with a Plan B. Great restaurants, spas, hot springs, Nordic trails, tubing, and other fun activities can keep a low-snow trip from feeling like a bust.
  • Insure the trip. Travel insurance can protect against some weather-related disruptions—if you splurge on a premium package and the “cancel for any reason” add-on. Make sure to read the fine print to understand if a disappointing snowpack is actually covered.

After Hours

Night skiing is the sport’s best-kept secret.

By Jenny Wiegand

Night skiing gets filed under “kid stuff,” something you age out of, like ski school and hot cocoa bribes. That’s backward. True, night skiing will never hand you the best run of your life. The snow’s usually chalky or refrozen, the lift is a single slow chair, and the terrain is lit like a parking lot. But none of that matters.

There’s a specific, illicit thrill to skiing after dark that goes back to childhood: staying up past bedtime on a school night, bombing down a green or blue run in a full, devil-may-care pizza, feeling like you’ve gotten away with something—you’re outside after dark in the heart of winter, and that alone makes it great.

It also makes you better. Strip a resort down to one run, and you stop sightseeing and start skiing—really skiing. It’s no coincidence that some of the best alpine skiers in the world were built on hills with just one lift and 300 feet of vertical.

If nothing else, it’s the only time you don’t have to wait in a lift line. Call it kid stuff if you want. Clearly, kids know something the rest of us forgot.

Fujifilm Announces New Pricing on its IMAX-Certified Flagship

Fujifilm has significantly lowered the entry price of its first dedicated cinema camera. The GFX ETERNA 55 is now listed at selected retailers in a body-only configuration for $9,999.95, approximately $6,500 less than its original package. The sensor, image processing, recording capabilities, and IMAX certification remain unchanged. More than a year after its official introduction, Fujifilm’s large-format cinema camera is entering a very different price segment. And that could be more significant than the price difference itself.

The same cinema camera for $6,500 less

When Fujifilm introduced the GFX ETERNA 55 in September 2025, its $16,500 price placed it firmly in the premium digital cinema category. As we reported in Fujifilm Officially Launches the GFX ETERNA 55: A $16,500 Medium Format Filmmaking Camera, this was Fujifilm’s first purpose-built cinema camera, combining its GFX sensor architecture with a professional filmmaking body. The new configuration, currently listed for preorder at selected retailers, excludes the following accessories:

  • Top handle
  • 5-inch touchscreen LCD monitor
  • LCD monitor hood
  • LCD monitor mount
  • External AC power adapter and cable

The battery and PL-mount adapter are still included. Importantly, the original $16,499 package remains available. This is not a conventional price reduction but a new purchasing option that lowers the entry cost by approximately 39%. For cinematographers who already own professional monitoring, power, and rigging solutions, the new configuration could make considerably more financial sense.

An IMAX-certified camera below $10,000

Perhaps the most interesting aspect of the new price is what happened between the camera’s original launch and today. In January 2026, the GFX ETERNA 55 officially joined the Filmed For IMAX program, as we covered in Meet the FUJIFILM ETERNA 55 IMAX Camera. The camera features a 43.8 × 32.9 mm sensor, offering approximately 67% more imaging area than a conventional full-frame sensor. Its native 4:3 Open Gate recording capability makes that additional sensor height particularly relevant for expanded aspect ratios, anamorphic cinematography, and immersive theatrical presentation. The combination of this sensor geometry and IMAX certification is now accessible through a camera body costing less than $10,000. That is a noteworthy development in the economics of premium digital cinema acquisition.

Does the new price change our previous assessment?

In October 2025, we published Fujifilm GFX ETERNA 55: Practical Considerations Before You Buy, examining the camera beyond its impressive sensor specifications. We identified several important considerations, including the absence of internal RAW recording, lens coverage requirements, rolling shutter performance, and the overall investment required to build a production-ready system. None of these considerations disappears with the new price. However, an additional $6,500 in the production budget can significantly change the equation. That money could instead be allocated toward cinema lenses, external RAW recording, monitoring, or a more comprehensive camera rig. The technical compromises remain the same. The financial compromises are now different.

Fujifilm is building a cinema ecosystem

The new pricing configuration also deserves to be examined alongside Fujifilm’s broader professional imaging strategy. In Fujifilm’s Imaging Business Is Booming, and GFX ETERNA 55 Points to Its Cinema Future, we analyzed how the company positions its first dedicated filmmaking camera within its expanding imaging business. More recently, Fujifilm announced a dedicated electronic viewfinder designed around the camera’s native sensor proportions, as detailed in Fujifilm Builds a 4:3 Cinema EVF Around the ETERNA 55 Open Gate Sensor. These developments suggest that Fujifilm is building a modular cinema platform rather than treating the ETERNA 55 as an isolated product. Offering the camera body independently is consistent with that approach.

Final thoughts

There is no publicly disclosed sales data indicating whether the original price limited adoption of the GFX ETERNA 55. Nevertheless, the new configuration represents a meaningful commercial repositioning. Fujifilm is now competing for owner-operators who might previously have excluded the ETERNA 55 from their purchasing decisions. A dedicated, IMAX-certified medium-format cinema camera with internal ProRes recording and electronic variable ND, priced below $10,000, is a compelling proposition. The camera has not changed. Its position in the cinema market has.

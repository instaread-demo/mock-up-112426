Closing Bell: ASX finally stops losing as Jensen presses CTRL+Z on Wall Street

News 12 hours ago Rob Badman

Jensen Huang got his old job back and Wall Street responded like somebody had discovered money.

Nvidia exploded 31% overnight after its board reinstated Huang as CEO less than two weeks after giving him the boot, adding almost US$1 trillion back to the chipmaker’s market value in a single session.

The Nasdaq jumped 6.5%, the S&P 500 surged 4.7% and the Dow gained 3%.

So, after eight straight days of being kicked in the groin, the ASX finally landed one of its own.

The benchmark jumped 1.82% on Tuesday, with 10 of 11 sectors higher and the Small Ords up 2.29%.

Tech led early after Wall Street’s AI eruption, while Materials kept climbing as copper and gold bounced overnight.

Copper gained about 3%, despite having fallen pretty much into a bear market only days ago. BHP (ASX:BHP) and Rio Tinto (ASX:RIO) joined in, along with a stack of local copper names.

Even the banks managed to turn up.

Energy was the sole miserable bastard, with oil sliding sharply as traders unwound some of the weekend’s Iran-war hedges.

So yes, after eight straight losses, the ASX appears to have remembered that green remains an available colour.

 

TLDR: Jensen got his swipe card back, Nvidia found nearly a trillion dollars behind the couch and the ASX finally stopped being a loser.

 

 

Here’s what else caught our eye…

  • Oil went the other way today, with Brent down more than 3% as Trump gave diplomacy with Iran a little more room.
  • Amazon reportedly paid US$7.5bn for AI startup Decart’s technology and talent. We checked and this seems to be the going rate these days for a few computers and a bunch of nerds.
  • And… scientists reckon there could be a hidden ninth planet lurking beyond Neptune, possibly around twice the size of Earth, after new modelling of some very weird distant orbits. Pluto has declined to comment.

 

Movers and Shakeouts

The ASX 200 finally broke the drought, jumping 1.82% to 8726.4 and snapping its eight-session losing streak.

It was a broad rebound, with 10 of the 11 sectors higher.

Tech led the charge, up 2.97%, while Consumer Discretionary rose 2.42%, Health Care gained 2.39% and Real Estate added 2.32%.

Industrials climbed 2.04%, Financials 1.84% and Materials 1.72%, while Staples, Telcos and Utilities also finished higher.

Energy was the only sector left sulking in the corner, down 0.17%.

The broader indices joined in, too, with the All Technology index up 2.91%, the All Ordinaries Gold index up 2.52%, the Small Ords up 2.29% and the ASX 200 Banks index up 1.82%.

 

TLDR: Ten sectors bounced. Energy just stood there, sweating, feeling awkward, pretending to look at its phone.

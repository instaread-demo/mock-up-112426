RuneScape players banish 1.56 billion premium items from the game, as Jagex continues its monetization purge

Jagex has continued its quest to cut down on RuneScape's microtransactions, allowing players to trade in bought items for in-game gold.

Lauren BerginNews Editor
Lauren spends most of her time dead in League of Legends, or equally as dead in Valorant. Don't ask her about Vampire: The Masquerade.
Published: November 24, 2026 5 hours ago

RuneScape has a long, convoluted history with microtransactions. With Jagex's monetization policies consistently coming under fire, it's been actively pulling back from aggressive MTXs as part of its Road to Restoration strategy. Earlier this year, the company removed Treasure Hunter - a paid system that allowed you to win daily prizes. Now players have banished 1.56 billion items from the game as part of the Well of Restoration quest, injecting 165 billion gold back into the ecosystem.

Well of Restoration's premise was pretty simple: drop one of myriad pre-selected, store-bought items into the well, get gold in return. The list was pretty extensive, and included everything from low-cost Protean and Unstable items, all the way up to the 200k Box of Deathtouched Darts and 100k Box of Superior Knowledge Bombs.

RuneScape's latest campaign, which ended on Monday, November 23, saw a total of 1.56 billion items purged, and in return, 165 billion gold pieces have been returned to players. Not bad at all.

This, in my mind, is a good thing. Monetization has, for better or worse, simply become a staple part of online videogames. While the tradeoff is normally that you play those games for free, RuneScape is subscription-based, meaning that you're not only paying to play, but also for in-game gear. While World of Warcraft's purchases are largely cosmetic, things like Treasure Hunter did feel like they gave some RuneScape players a marked advantage over others. It's a good thing, then, that Jagex is pulling back on a lot of its more P2W elements - especially ahead of RuneScape 4.

Coming into December, Jagex is releasing RuneScape's Remastered Journey, which retouches everything from gameplay to graphics. Alongside that drop, there's the Adventurer's Guide, which will help guide new players from A to B with ease, and a ten-day free trial period where you'll be able to access current members' worlds. That runs from Wednesday, December 2, all the way until Saturday, December 12, so if you're looking for a new MMO obsession, now's the time to get involved.

Some of the biggest games of all time are celebrating milestone anniversaries in November 2026

While some big new games are launching in November 2026, some of the biggest games of the last 20 years are celebrating big anniversaries.

SponsoredIn association with

While November 2026 sees the release of Grand Theft Auto 6, a game we'll be celebrating for a long time, it's also the time to celebrate milestones in the history of some of the biggest game series of all time.

One of the most iconic RPGs ever released, The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, released 15 years ago. Famously released on 11/11/11, Skyrim has become a game you can play wherever you like. The original versions hold up; upgraded versions for modern consoles and PCs make it look prettier, you can immerse yourself in virtual reality versions, and there are even refrigerators you can load it on up.

From famous lines, instantly recognisable characters, the iconic opening, there are things from Skyrim that'll live on, even once the next Elder Scrolls game finally releases.

While the original version of the game is a masterpiece, it's the evolution of Skyrim and what players can do in the game's world that have made it stand the test of time. The almost endless suite of mod options available for the game means you can tweak the experience to something new in pretty much any way you can imagine. You can overhaul the world, make some quality of life changes, or add something inherently silly. Skyrim is a classic of the RPG genre, and will continue to influence games far into the future.

Another huge game celebrating its 15th anniversary this month is Minecraft. One of the biggest-selling games of all time is still going strong today, thanks in part to the updates that continue to be released, adding new features for people to dive into.

It's great that the game can be taken wherever you're going, too. While it's at its best on PC, you can play on pretty much any console, your tablet, and even your phone, letting you tweak your world on the go if you'd like.

Whether you want to hang out with your friends, build absolute masterpieces over the course of years, or just run away from some zombies, Minecraft lets you do whatever you'd like.

It's another game that gives you the freedom to make the experience match whatever you'd like, too. You can load up other players' seeds to find the best world for what you need, you can install mods to make the game easier to navigate, or you can make the world look better with countless available shaders. Minecraft will continue to be huge, and G2A.COM is a perfect place to go to rediscover it.

Stepping away from some of the biggest games of all time, there are some other beloved games celebrating this month. It has been 10 years since the release of Dishonored 2. We're still waiting for Arkane's next game to release, but the Dishonored sequel is arguably their best yet, standing up well today.

We don't want to spoil the story for people who haven't played it, but the game stands out thanks to its incredible atmosphere. The world you explore, trying to silently take people out as you go, is dark but caricatured, making for something unique in videogames.

While nothing has changed since launch, and it's not one that'll change with loads of mods like Skyrim and Minecraft, it's well worth going back to today. The gameplay, style, and story are all among the best in an action game in the last decade.

If you're looking for something sillier, the highlight of the Saints Row series, The Third, turns 15 this month. In many ways, it's similar to GTA, somehow going even further with its over-the-top slapstick action.

In a series that has had its fair share of ups and downs, The Third does the best job balancing the absurd with good gameplay. The personality shines, but it's still fun to play. Other Saints Row games have been crazy but a chore to play, but The Third gets it just right.

If you're not diving into GTA 6 because it's not coming to PC, Saints Row The Third would be the perfect next best option.

November is always a big month for gaming anniversaries, and this month is no different. If you want to return to the likes of Minecraft, Skyrim, and Dishonored 2, or you've never played them before, G2A.COM is the perfect place to go to pick them up.

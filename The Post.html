Large-scale solar farm developer in receivership

Claire Taylor|Local Democracy Reporting
November 23, 2026 • 11:19pm

The company behind a proposed large-scale solar farm in the Mackenzie has been placed into receivership, following a suspension of the project last month.

Far North Solar Farm Ltd (FNSF), a renewable energy developer directed by Richard Homewood and Melbourne-based John Telfer, had sought fast-track consent to build a 40MW solar farm, known as The Point, in the Mackenzie District.

The company has now been placed into receivership by Aquila Clean Energy SG Holdco Pte. Ltd, according to a receivership notice, with Stephen Keen and Stephen White of Teneo Financial Advisory New Zealand appointed joint receivers on November 18 pursuant to a General Security Deed.

The deed pertained to a joint venture between the two companies, with Aquila taking control of FNSF’s assets to recover money owed under a 2021 agreement.

The venture, which commissioned the Pukenui solar farm in Northland, was disbanded in January, with Aquila’s website saying it was to enable both parties to “pursue their perspective strategic priorities independently”.

AD

The website said the companies agreed to split ownership of proposed projects, with FNSF taking developments in Greytown, the Mackenzie, and other areas of the South Island.

This follows a contentious period for the Mackenzie community after FNSF filed a fast-track application to develop the proposed solar farm along the northern shore of Lake Benmore last October.

In August, Homewood told the fast-track panel FNSF would not pay its outstanding invoices, stating non-payment was the only way to secure more planning time ahead of an August 31 deadline for expert ecological information.

The Environmental Protection Authority subsequently suspended the application, with no timeframe set for repayment.

At the time, an FNSF spokesperson defended the move saying the company intended to pay its fees and progress the project.

However, Mary Murdoch, a spokesperson for a Twizel community group working to educate residents on developments in the district, accused the developer of misusing the legislation.

“They do appear to be gaming the system and not using it as intended,” Murdoch said in October.

FNSF firmly denied the characterisation.

The working group first met in August to discuss the wave of proposed solar farms for the district, led by Murdoch.

Residents had discussed the introduction of specific land overlays to protect areas of natural landscape, and of forming sustainability coalitions as seen in Southland and Queenstown.

“[A] sustainable growth coalition might be the way to go. Small groups doing research and getting independent expert advice to put to the fast-track panel,” Murdoch said.

“People are not happy about what’s going on,” she said.

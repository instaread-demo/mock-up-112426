Arizona ranks 6th-worst in US for drunk driving, study finds

Out of four categories, Arizona ranked higher than the national average in each one.

If you’re planning on crisscrossing the Valley this holiday season to spend time with family (or argue with them), it’s a good idea to be extra vigilant on the roads. Arizona has one of the highest drunk-driving rates in the nation, according to a ConsumerAffairs report. 

Arizona is the sixth-worst state for driving drunk in the country, with South Carolina, Montana and Wyoming leading the ignominious list. The consumer review and news platform ranked the 50 states’ drunk-driving rates by analyzing the most recent data from 2024 on fatal car crashes involving driving drunk and DUI arrests from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, the FBI and the U.S. Census Bureau.

“Arizona stood out probably in the worst ways,” said Jailyn Rodriguez, a strategist for ConsumerAffairs. “It ranked so high on our list because of not only the drunk drivers involved in deadly crashes, but also separately the people killed in those crashes.” 

Out of four categories, Arizona ranked higher than the national average in each one. Law enforcement makes nearly 280 DUI arrests for every 100,000 Arizonans. That rate is 81 more arrests than the national average. That high rate of DUI arrests translates to a lot of drunk drivers on Arizona’s highways and side streets, resulting in avoidable crashes and even deaths.

Of the traffic deaths in the state, 34.1% involved drunk drivers. That’s 4 percentage points higher than the national average. In fatal crashes per 100,000 residents, drunk drivers were involved in more than 5 in Arizona — more than 2 higher than the national average.

Similarly, there are 5.5 drunk-driving fatalities in Arizona per 100,000 residents. That rate is 2 fatalities per 100,000 higher than the national average.

While drunk driving was involved in 37% of traffic deaths over Thanksgiving nationwide in 2024, it appears that drunk-driving crashes don’t spike on Thanksgiving in Arizona, according to a study by AZDUI.org. 

Compared to drunk-driving fatal crash rates on other holidays, alcohol-related fatal crashes occur less on Thanksgiving, the study found. The Arizona Department of Transportation reported that from 2019 to 2024, there were 20 alcohol-related fatal crashes during the holiday’s reporting window. That total is 1.5 crashes lower than AZDUI.org’s prediction, based on estimated crashes for the same days and hours from historical reporting. 

Still, don’t take your eyes off the road yet. Arizona’s Thanksgiving had the same number of alcohol-related fatal crashes as New Year’s in that time period — which was the highest of the six holidays analyzed. So it’s best to remain vigilant this holiday season. 

“The potential for drunk driving is always there year-round,” Rodriguez said. “But we can assume, because of all the get-togethers happening, that maybe the chances are a little bit higher during the holidays.”

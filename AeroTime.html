Russian warplanes trigger unprecedented air raid alert in South Korea

ByClement Charpentreau
November 24, 2026, 14:14 GMT+02:00
3 minute read

South Korea issued an unprecedented air raid alert for Ulleung County on November 24, 2026, after several Russian military aircraft entered the Korea Air Defense Identification Zone (KADIZ) over the Sea of Japan, known in South Korea as the East Sea, and approached the island of Ulleungdo, according to the country’s Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS). 

The JCS said it had detected the Russian aircraft before they entered the zone and that they subsequently turned away without violating South Korean territorial airspace. The Republic of Korea Air Force (ROKAF) scrambled fighter jets in response, with South Korean media reporting that F-15Ks were among the aircraft deployed. 

Local media citing military officials put the number of Russian aircraft at fewer than five. The JCS did not identify the aircraft types or disclose how long they remained in the zone or how close they came to South Korean territorial airspace. 

Residents ordered to take shelter 

At the military’s request, South Korea’s Ministry of the Interior and Safety issued an air raid alert at 07:52 local time. A minute later, it sent an emergency text message instructing residents to seek shelter underground or indoors. The message incorrectly reported that Russian aircraft had violated South Korean airspace. The alert was lifted within five minutes. 

Further confusion arose when North Gyeongsang Province’s all-clear message referred to a suspected North Korean aircraft. Provincial authorities said they had mistakenly reused a template from a civil defense drill, changing only the time. 

JCS spokesperson Captain Jang Do-young said the Russian aircraft had come so close to Ulleungdo’s territorial airspace that the military anticipated a violation and acted preemptively. 

According to Yonhap News Agency, it was the first time South Korea had issued a preliminary air raid alert in anticipation of a possible airspace violation by Russian military aircraft. 

Seoul summons Russian defense attaché 

South Korea’s Ministry of National Defense summoned Russia’s defense attaché, Colonel Nikolai Marchenko, on the afternoon of November 24, 2026. Kim Hak-jo, the ministry’s director general for international policy, lodged a formal protest and urged Moscow to prevent a recurrence. 

The ministry described the repeated unannounced entries into the KADIZ and the latest close approach as unacceptable, saying they could “unnecessarily raise tensions on the Korean Peninsula and in the region.” 

Marchenko declined to comment when approached by reporters. South Korea’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs also expressed regret to Moscow. 

Separately, the Russian Ministry of Defense announced on November 24, 2026, that Tu-160 strategic bombers had completed a planned flight lasting approximately 10 hours over international waters of the Sea of Japan, escorted by Su-35S and Su-30SM fighters. 

The Russian statement made no mention of South Korea, and Seoul has not confirmed whether those aircraft were involved in the incident. 

Unlike territorial airspace, an air defense identification zone can extend over international airspace, where authorities request approaching aircraft to identify themselves for national security purposes. 

Third Russian KADIZ entry in eight days 

The incident followed two other Russian military aircraft entries into the KADIZ on November 17 and 19, 2026, reported by the JCS. 

On November 17, 2026, Japan’s Ministry of Defense reported that two suspected Russian aircraft had briefly violated Japanese airspace northwest of Okinoshima, off Fukuoka Prefecture. On November 19, 2026, Japan’s Joint Staff tracked two Tu-95 strategic bombers and two Tu-142 maritime patrol aircraft flying through the Tsushima Strait. 

Russian military aircraft also entered the KADIZ on August 25, 2026, and during a joint strategic air patrol with China on June 27, 2026.  

One of the most serious incidents occurred on July 23, 2019, when ROKAF F-15Ks fired warning shots at a Russian A-50 airborne early warning and control aircraft that Seoul said had twice violated its territorial airspace near the Dokdo islets. Moscow denied the violations.

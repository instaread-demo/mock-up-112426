Human Rights Watch raises fresh concerns over migrant pushbacks at Croatia’s borders

In a report published on Tuesday, 24 November, the international human rights organisation said its research had found continued cases of people being forcibly returned from Croatia to Bosnia and Herzegovina, often involving violence and inadequate access to asylum procedures.

The 43-page report, titled Doing Europe’s Dirty Work: Ongoing Violent Pushbacks of Migrants from Croatia to Bosnia and Herzegovina, argues that existing monitoring arrangements have failed to prevent the alleged abuses.

What does the report say?

Human Rights Watch said it interviewed more than 80 people, including seven unaccompanied children, four children travelling with family members and six parents travelling with their children.

Those interviewed described alleged incidents involving physical assaults, confiscation of money and mobile phones, destruction of personal documents and belongings, and forced returns across the border.

Some also reported being forced to cross rivers or walk through remote areas after being returned to Bosnia and Herzegovina.

The organisation said it had found evidence of repeated pushbacks, including cases involving families separated during operations.

It also cited figures from Save the Children, which recorded 1,811 accounts of people being pushed back from Croatia to Bosnia and Herzegovina between August 2024 and October 2025. These included 276 cases affecting children.

Concerns over European Union monitoring

The report also questions the effectiveness of the EU-funded monitoring mechanism established to oversee Croatia’s border operations.

Human Rights Watch argues that the mechanism has had limited impact and that inadequate oversight, ineffective investigations and a lack of accountability have allowed the alleged practices to continue.

The organisation has called on Croatian authorities to end pushbacks, ensure people can access asylum procedures and establish independent monitoring.

It has also called on the European Commission to make border-management funding conditional on demonstrable improvements.

Croatian authorities reject the allegations

The Croatian Ministry of the Interior provided a response to Human Rights Watch, denying that Croatian police engage in pushbacks.

According to the report, the ministry said people arriving in Croatia have access to international protection procedures and that police have received guidance and training on implementing the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum.

The report is the latest development in a long-running debate over Croatia’s management of its external EU border and its obligations under European and international asylum law.
